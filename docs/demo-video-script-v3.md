This is LotteryClock, an alarm clock built for Kiroween, for the Resurrection and Costume Contest tracks.

It all started many moons ago, when young Dylan stopped trusting his alarm clock.

He was always late for school, because he always hit snooze. One day, while cleaning his room, he tripped over the cord and unplugged the clock. He plugged it back in, but he didn't know how long it had been off.

The next morning the alarm went off, and he stopped before hitting snooze. Was the time right? Was he already late?

He didn't know, so he got up. He was on time for the next few days, until he worked out the difference and fixed the clock.

In 2012, Dylan tried to turn the idea into an iPhone app. It never shipped. Now, for Kiroween, he's brought it back to life.

Here's how it works. Set the time you want to wake up, say 7:30. Then choose how much risk you want, from 5 to 45 minutes. With 15 minutes, the alarm will ring at a random time between 7:15 and 7:45.

When you tap Set Alarm, the iPhone app schedules a real alarm at that random time. You don't know when it will go off.

If it rings early, you win, and you can snooze until 7:30. If it rings late, you lose, and it's time to get up.

Either way, you have to check which one it is, and by then you're awake.

The themes are the costumes. The same alarm comes in seven looks, including Good Night, Día de Muertos, Ouija and Tarot, each with its own art, sounds and wording.

You can also try it in your browser at LotteryClock dot app.

Good luck.
